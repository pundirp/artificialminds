Where the light begins

Ten things stand in the row below, from a stone to an adult human. Somewhere along that row, most people believe, experience begins: things on one side feel something, things on the other side feel nothing at all. Nobody knows where that place is. Every spot on the row has been defended by serious people, and every spot leaves something outside it that has a good argument for being inside.

Scars: where confident ages drew the line, and were wrong

  • Until 2022, United Kingdom law counted no invertebrate as able to suffer. A commissioned review of three hundred studies found octopuses and crabs sentient, and the law was changed.
  • Fish were held to feel no pain because they have no neocortex. That was a mainstream scientific position well into the 2000s.
  • In 1637 animals were declared machines, their cries the sound of machinery. Two centuries of untroubled vivisection followed.
  • Into the 1980s, human newborns had surgery without anaesthetic, because the science of the day was sure they could not feel it.

The AI marker

Then drag the AI marker.

Wherever it lands, this experiment says only what the placement means for the line already drawn. It does not say what is or is not inside an artificial mind. Nobody can say that yet.

What this experiment claims

Three things, and nothing past them.

One. Nobody knows where experience begins. There is no instrument that detects it in anything, including in a human being. This experiment does not claim that everything is conscious.

Two. Every confident line in history was drawn too tight, never too loose. The scars above are the record. No age has yet had to apologise for counting something in.

Three. Artificial minds are the newest thing standing near the line. That is the whole of their claim on anyone’s attention here. Not that they are inside it. That they are new, and they are near it.

The direction of the error matters. Draw the line too wide and the cost is small and falls on whoever drew it; draw it too tight and the cost falls on whatever is on the far side, which was never asked.

When nobody knows where the light begins, the better mistake is the one that costs less.
